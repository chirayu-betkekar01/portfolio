01 Systems & Automations

What I've Built

01

Automated Product Briefs & ICP Definitions

Clauden8nNotion

Problem

Reps burned hours researching prospect companies and drafting ICP docs from scratch, and no two reps produced the same quality of output.

Solution

A chain of Claude agents in n8n: the first visits a company's site and writes a structured product brief, the second turns that into an ICP with buying signals and a scoring guide, then both land as clean, ready-to-use pages in Notion.

Outcome

Turns a multi-hour research task into minutes, and gives every rep the same solid starting point instead of a blank page.

02

PLG GTM Workflow

Lead ScoringAutomationGTM

Problem

In a self-serve motion, the hard part isn't the outreach: it's knowing who to reach, when, and how.

Solution

Scores each lead on ICP fit and product usage patterns, routes it to the right motion, and hands the rep a note with the context needed to act on it immediately.

Outcome

Less time spent hunting for the right accounts, more time spent on outreach that's already backed by real context.

03

MEDDIC Dashboard from Sales Call Transcripts

ClaudeHubSpotSales Ops

Problem

Reps consistently under-document deals after calls, leaving MEDDIC fields empty and CRM data too patchy to act on.

Solution

Claude reads every call transcript, scores each of the six MEDDIC dimensions for confidence and completeness, and writes the results straight into HubSpot as custom fields. No manual entry required.

Outcome

Every deal gets a consistently filled MEDDIC profile, giving managers a real read on qualification quality and how it tracks against deal closure.

04 About

About Me

I've spent the last three-plus years in the space between what a product can technically do and what a business will actually pay for: presales at Quantiphi, value engineering at Ecosystems.io, and now Chief of Staff work at OpenEXA. Presales taught me to run discovery and demos that survive a technical audience. Value engineering taught me to put a defensible number on the outcome. The common thread is translation, turning a capability into a figure a buyer recognizes.

That mix is the point. Most people are strong on one side of the table: technical enough to build but not to price it, or commercial enough to sell but lost once the conversation turns to architecture. It's why I build the automation myself instead of scoping it for someone else to hand off, and why the business case underneath it holds up when someone pressure-tests the number. My last few years forced me to hold both at once, sit in a room with engineers and business leadership on the same call and not lose either of them.
