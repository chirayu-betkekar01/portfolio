04Engage stage

Call-to-CRM Agent Pipeline

Watch walkthrough GitHub
ClaudeFathomHubSpot

Problem

Reps take calls with several stakeholders, but only the person they invited ends up in the CRM. The decision maker who joined from a conference room, or was only mentioned, never gets logged, and nobody labels buying roles by hand.

Solution

Three Claude agents work from the Fathom call transcript. The first works out who was really on or named in the call. The second tags Decision Makers, Champions and Blockers from how people behave, keeping a role only when its evidence quote is in the transcript. The third fills in missing contact details and syncs everyone to the deal in HubSpot, without overwriting existing data or creating deals.

Outcome

Every call leaves a complete, labeled buying group on the deal with no manual entry from reps, and every role traces back to a line in the transcript.

How it works

Fathom call transcript Invitees, speakers and everyone mentioned
Agent 1 · Who was on the call Including people who were never invited
Agent 2 · Buying roles Kept only when the evidence quote is in the transcript Decision MakerChampionBlocker
Agent 3 · Enrich and sync Fills empty fields in HubSpot, never creates deals
Labeled buying group On the deal, no manual entry
Traceable roles Each one cites a line from the call

06Engage stage

RFP Bid Qualification

Live demo GitHub
ClaudeNext.js

Problem

Before a team commits weeks to an RFP response, someone has to split the document into requirements, chase the right expert for each one, and decide whether it's worth bidding at all, usually on gut feel.

Solution

Claude splits the RFP into line items by section and suggests Yes, No or Maybe for each from the approved answer library, citing the answers it used. The proposal manager routes items to experts, who answer through a personal link with the suggestion shown but never pre-selected. Code then scores the answers against fixed go/no-go rules, flags hard fails like a mandatory HIPAA BAA, and the manager makes the call with a written reason.

Outcome

Every bid decision rests on a score that traces back to an expert's answer and a line in the RFP, and the AI never makes the call. On the City of Tulsa's public CRM RFP, extraction caught 96% of the requirements in a 94-row answer key.

How it works

RFP document Parsed with section and page references
Claude splits and suggests One line item per obligation, each with a Yes, No or Maybe from the library MandatoryGood to HaveOptional
Experts answer Personal links, with a note on every No or Maybe
Go/no-go score Fixed rules out of 100, plus hard-fail checks
Bid or no-bid The manager decides, with a written reason

04 · About

About Me

I've spent the last three-plus years in the space between what a product can technically do and what a business will actually pay for: presales at Quantiphi, value engineering at Ecosystems.io, Chief of Staff work at OpenEXA, and now AI consulting at Database Republic. Presales taught me to run discovery and demos that survive a technical audience. Value engineering taught me to put a defensible number on the outcome. Consulting is where I build it end to end: automation pipelines that connect a client's systems, with LLMs drafting the follow-ups. The common thread is translation, turning a capability into a figure a buyer recognizes.

That mix is the point. Most people are strong on one side of the table: technical enough to build but not to price it, or commercial enough to sell but lost once the conversation turns to architecture. It's why I build the automation myself instead of scoping it for someone else to hand off, and why the business case underneath it holds up when someone pressure-tests the number. My last few years forced me to hold both at once, sit in a room with engineers and business leadership on the same call and not lose either of them.
